SRTDrive Safety-Aware Trajectory Refinement via Reference-Relative Supervision

A frozen end-to-end planner can output a plan that collides when a small adjustment would not. SRTDrive keeps that plan as a reference, generates alternatives as deformations of it, and learns when to keep it and when to replace it.

Abstract

End-to-end planners output a single final plan that can still collide with surrounding agents, even when a small adjustment could avoid the collision. Retraining the planner changes its weights and acquired behavior, and learned candidate scorers are not trained to judge a replacement relative to the original plan.

We propose SRTDrive, a post-hoc refinement framework that leaves the planner frozen, keeps its output unchanged as a reference trajectory, and learns whether to retain or replace it. A generator produces alternatives as deformations of the reference along and across its path, so that every alternative is directly comparable to the original plan. A selector then scores each candidate relative to the reference and is trained so that a replacement that increases collision at any horizon is never the target, however close it is to the logged trajectory; trajectory error only ranks equally safe candidates. At inference, the top-ranked candidate, possibly the reference itself, is output without a collision threshold.

On Bench2Drive, SRTDrive raises the Driving Score of VAD-Base from 43.07 to 47.77 and reduces the routes with collisions from 95 to 86, whereas the same candidates selected by a TTC rule do not yield these gains. On nuScenes, applied to seven frozen planners with one module trained per planner, it lowers L2 error on five and the collision rate on three, with the largest gains on VAD.

How it works

The planner is never retrained. Its output serves twice: as the anchor that alternatives are generated around, and as the baseline every candidate is scored against.

  1. Deform the reference

    A rectified-flow generator produces seven alternatives as deformations of the reference: progress along its path, plus normal and tangential offsets. Every alternative stays directly comparable to the original plan.

  2. Keep the reference in the set

    The planner's trajectory joins the candidates unchanged. It is scored as one candidate, not assumed to be safe.

  3. Score relative to the reference

    A set-contextual selector attends across candidates and computes every logit from a candidate–reference pair. The top-ranked candidate is output as is.

Collision comes first

The selector learns a strict order: a later criterion only breaks ties left by the one before it.

  1. No worse than the referenceCollision does not increase at any of the 1, 2 and 3 s horizons.
  2. Minimum collisionAmong those, the lowest average collision.
  3. Closest to the logged trajectoryDisplacement error only ranks equally safe candidates.

Limits

What the evidence does not show.

  • The priority is learned, not enforced

    The collision-first order is a learned preference with no threshold behind it at inference. At 3 s, 2 of 3,987 replacements on nuScenes raise the collision metric.

  • Selection is independent at each step

    With VAD-Base, consecutive plans vary more and closed-loop Comfort falls from 50.80% to 40.26%. The analysis does not establish that one causes the other.

  • Alternatives stay near the reference

    Deformations cannot express a different route. Overtaking success drops on both planners: 20.00% to 11.11% on VAD-Base and 71.11% to 66.67% on BevAD-M.

  • Gains are not uniform

    Open-loop nuScenes results are mixed across planners, and NAVSIM v2 with VADv2 shows no aggregate gain (82.167 to 82.166, non-reactive). Planners with unfavorable open-loop changes were not evaluated in closed loop.
